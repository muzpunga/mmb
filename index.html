Visual inspection of Q-Q plots and boxplots indicated only minor departures from normality. Skewness was low in the Interim group (0.25) and moderate in the MMB group (-0.81). Given the robustness of Welch’s independent-samples t-test to moderate non-normality and unequal variances, EPDS scores were compared using a Welch t-test.

Mean_Difference CI_Lower CI_Upper t df p

Mean EPDS scores did not differ significantly between the Interim and MMB groups. A Welch independent-samples t-test indicated that the observed mean difference of -0.50 points (Interim: M = 14.4, SD = 3.3; MMB: M = 14.9, SD = 4.2) was not statistically significant, t(54.54) = -0.96, p = .341. The 95% confidence interval for the mean difference ranged from -1.55 to 0.54, indicating that the true difference could plausibly range from a small advantage for either group.

The number of distinct sessions visited was analysed using negative binomial regression. A Poisson model initially demonstrated overdispersion (dispersion = 1.93), indicating that the Poisson assumption of equal mean and variance was violated. A negative binomial model substantially improved model fit, reducing the dispersion statistic to 1.04, suggesting an adequate fit to the data.

Participants in the Interim group visited more distinct sessions on average (M = 3.0, SD = 2.0, Median = 2.0, n = 45) than participants in the MMB group (M = 1.2, SD = 1.5, Median = 1.0, n = 647). Negative binomial regression indicated that the MMB group had a significantly lower rate of session attendance than the Interim group, β = -0.93, SE = 0.16, z = -5.92, p < .001.

The number of days visited was analysed using negative binomial regression. A Poisson model exhibited substantial overdispersion (dispersion = 8.33), indicating poor fit. Although some overdispersion remained in the negative binomial model (dispersion = 2.21), model comparison statistics favoured the negative binomial model over a zero-inflated negative binomial model (AIC = 2693.65 vs. 2697.65; BIC = 2707.27 vs. 2720.35), and the Vuong test also supported the negative binomial model.

Participants in the Interim group visited the platform on more days (M = 6.1, SD = 5.3, median = 4.0, n = 45) than participants in the MMB group (M = 2.0, SD = 4.1, median = 1.0, n = 647). Negative binomial regression indicated that the MMB group had a significantly lower rate of days visited than the Interim group, β = -1.13, SE = 0.19, z = -5.88, p < .001.

The number of distinct articles visited was analysed using negative binomial regression. A Poisson model demonstrated substantial overdispersion (dispersion = 3.85), which was adequately addressed by the negative binomial model (dispersion = 0.76). Participants in the Interim group visited more distinct articles (M = 1.6, SD = 2.2, median = 0.0, n = 45) than participants in the MMB group (M = 0.5, SD = 1.4, median = 0.0, n = 647). Negative binomial regression indicated that article visitation rates were significantly lower in the MMB group than in the Interim group, β = -1.11, SE = 0.44, z = -2.52, p = .012. Participants in the MMB group visited articles at approximately 33% of the rate observed in the Interim group (IRR = 0.33), equivalent to the Interim group visiting approximately 3.0 times as many articles.

5 Sensitivity Analysis

Four prespecified outcomes were examined (EPDS, number of distinct sessions visited, number of days visited, and number of distinct articles visited). A Holm correction for multiple comparisons was applied as a sensitivity analysis. The pattern of results was unchanged: differences in distinct sessions visited (adjusted p < .001), days visited (adjusted p < .001), and articles visited (adjusted p = .023) remained statistically significant, whereas the difference in EPDS scores remained non-significant (adjusted p = .341).
